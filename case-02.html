02 / THE PROBLEMSpend without leads, and no way to tell why

A family-owned flooring showroom was running generic campaigns that mixed every product together. Four of the five legacy campaigns had spent over $2,000 combined with zero recorded leads.

Part of the problem was the ads. The bigger part was measurement: phone calls and contact forms weren't being counted as leads, so Google's bidding had nothing to learn from and kept buying cheap clicks.

Old setup vs new setup, same May–Aug window
Old: 4 campaigns, 0 leads on 4 of 5 legacy campaigns. New: 3 campaigns, 31 tracked leads.
  • Products were mixed in one campaign, so a tile shopper could land on a carpet ad and page.
  • Bidding was set to Maximize Clicks, which rewards traffic, not customers.
  • Calls and forms were undercounted, so there was no proof of what worked.

03 / WHAT I DIDStep by step

Step 1 · Restructure

Rebuilt the account around one campaign per service

I replaced the mixed campaigns with service-specific campaigns and ad groups: carpet, luxury vinyl and laminate, tile, and hardwood. Each one has its own landing page, ad copy and keywords.

  • 15 headlines and 4 descriptions per ad group, written for that product only.
  • 30 exact and 30 phrase match keywords per service, all high intent.
  • Shared sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets and a lead form built around the free in-home estimate.
  • A 15-mile radius around the showroom, with higher bids on the five highest-value zip codes.
Step 2 · Tracking (June 29)

Fixed call and form tracking

I set phone calls and contact-form submissions up as proper lead conversions so every real enquiry was counted. Before this, Google could not tell a lead from a bounce.

Step 3 · Bidding (July 13)

Switched from Maximize Clicks to Maximize Conversions

With leads now tracked, I told Google to optimize for leads instead of traffic. Clicks went down. Leads went up.

4 leads→15 leadsTile Flooring · Jun 10–Jul 7 vs Jul 8–Aug 4 · +275%
Step 4 · Diagnosis

Proved the July slowdown was a budget ceiling, not lost demand

Clicks and impressions dropped after July 17. I matched Google Ads against Google Analytics: both showed the same drop at the same time, which ruled out a tracking glitch. The cause was the campaigns hitting their daily caps. All three showed "Limited" status.

Step 5 · Recommendation

Wrote the budget case the owner approved

I wrote a one-page performance review with a clear answer: yes, spend more, but in phases and starting where the data was strongest.

  • Tile first: proven at $42.68 a lead and capped by budget, so the lowest-risk place to add spend.
  • Hardwood next, once keyword refinements brought its cost per lead closer to tile.
  • Carpet: expand keywords before adding money, because it was limited by keywords, not budget.
  • Review cost per lead at each step over 30 to 60 days rather than one big jump.
Step 6 · Expansion

Added 82 keywords and scaled the budget

When the owner asked about vinyl and laminate, I added 82 keywords (waterproof, pet-friendly, wood-look and basement searches) and set budgets across four service campaigns.

3 campaigns→$98/dayTile $40 · Hardwood $21 · Carpet $21 · Luxury vinyl $16 · about $2,980/month
