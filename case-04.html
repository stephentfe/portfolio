02 / THE PROBLEMReactivating an account that was counting the wrong things

An industrial chemical manufacturer selling into the US, Canada and Latin America had paused all 17 Google Ads campaigns during a website problem. When I audited the account in August, the analytics tag had stopped sending data, several Performance Max asset groups were disapproved for "destination not working", and Analytics had no data from mid-May to August 10.

Once tracking was back and campaigns restarted, the quarterly numbers looked excellent: nearly 400 conversions. Digging into GA4 showed why they were wrong.

Lead events vs verified enquiries, one quarter
  • The generate_lead event fired 382 times from 117 users, while only 32 people even started a form.
  • 255 of those events came from two Portuguese-language landing pages whose form received 1 submission all quarter.
  • A "contact us" conversion fired on any visit to the contact page, not on a submission.
  • Every page load sent two page views: one from the Tag Manager tag and one from the site builder's own Google Ads integration.
  • Phone and email conversions could never fire because the site had no clickable phone or email links.

03 / WHAT I DIDStep by step

Step 1 · Reactivation audit (August)

Recommended reactivating, not rebuilding

I audited all 17 campaigns, the Google tag, GA4 and the disapproved assets. The account wasn't suspended and had real history, so I recommended a sequence: restore the tag, fix the broken landing page URLs, resubmit the disapproved assets, then unpause the strongest campaigns first.

Step 2 · Duplicate page views

Stopped every page view from counting twice

I set the Tag Manager Google tag to stop sending its own page view, leaving the site builder's one in place, then confirmed each page load sends a single page view.

2 per load→1 per loadGA4 page_view · fixed in Tag Manager and verified live
Step 3 · Inflated leads

Took the inflated event out of the lead count

I moved the website's generate_lead event to secondary in Google Ads so it no longer counts as a lead or steers bidding.

Step 4 · Real lead tracking

Installed tracking that only fires on real leads

  • lead_form_submit fires only when the form's success message appears.
  • phone_click and email_click fire on tel: and mailto: link clicks.
  • Made the footer phone number a clickable link on every page so phone leads can be measured at all.
  • Tested live: each event reached GA4 after accepting cookies in a test browser.
Step 5 · Explaining the gaps

Showed why clicks and sessions didn't match

Search ads produced only 18 to 20 sessions per 100 clicks. I traced it to the cookie banner: analytics stays off until a visitor clicks "Accept all", so most paid visits are never recorded. I also found that 5,824 of one campaign's 5,872 clicks came from YouTube placements.

Step 6 · Reporting

Added tracking-status slides to the quarterly deck

I added three slides to the quarterly report (tracking status, Google Ads clicks vs website sessions, and Performance Max channels) and switched the top-pages slide from views to users, so leadership could see exactly what each number meant.
