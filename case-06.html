02 / THE PROBLEMLead numbers nobody could trust

A lawn and outdoor services company runs a seasonal business: mowing and fertilization in spring and summer, aeration, irrigation shut-off and holiday lighting in the fall. The ad account was reporting far more leads than the client's back office.

  • Two Analytics conversions switched on in April (form submit and phone tap) were counting leads already captured by the website and call tracking.
  • In July, Google Ads showed 73 form leads against 65 in the back office. The primary form action counted every submission rather than one per visit.
  • A "Submit lead form" action fired on page load, so simply visiting the page could register as a lead.
  • The client was billed more than expected in July and assumed the campaigns had overspent.

03 / WHAT I DIDStep by step

Step 1 · June

Removed the double-counting conversions

I set the two April Analytics conversions to secondary. They still record, but no longer add to the lead total. The original website form and call tracking stayed primary, so bidding carried on unchanged.

2 duplicates→0Primary lead actions counting the same enquiry twice
Step 2 · Reporting

Pointed lead reporting to the back office

While the counts were being cleaned up, I flagged that monthly report lead figures should come from the client's back office rather than Google Ads, so the report wouldn't overstate results.

Step 3 · Fall pivot

Rebuilt the account around four fall services

  • Added negative keywords to filter out mowing searches in Search and Performance Max.
  • Paused four off-season ad groups: weed control, mosquito control, irrigation startup and lawn fertilization.
  • Built three new ad groups (overseeding & aeration, holiday lighting, irrigation winterization), each with 90 keywords, 15 headlines and 4 descriptions.
  • Rewrote Performance Max headlines, long headlines and descriptions to lead with the fall services.

Step 4 · Judgment call

Flagged a strong performer that fell outside the brief

Lawn fertilization wasn't one of the four requested fall services, but it was performing well. I paused it as briefed and flagged it straight away. The client chose to keep it, so I switched it back on, with the mowing negatives already protecting it from the jobs they didn't want.

Step 5 · Billing

Explained a billing surprise with the statement

The July invoice was higher than the client expected. I checked the statement: the Search and Performance Max campaigns had billed $809.73, under the roughly $1,010 monthly budget. The overage was Local Services Ads, $1,386.47 for 30 leads, a separate pay-per-lead Google product. I recommended the client either cap it or turn it off.

Step 6 · September–October

Fixed the remaining counting issues

  • Found why Ads showed 73 forms against 65: the primary action counted every submission, not one per visit.
  • Set the GA4 form-submit action to primary and the page-load "Submit lead form" action to secondary.
  • Checked September's 106 form leads and confirmed they were believable.
