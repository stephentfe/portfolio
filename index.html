CASE 01
SearchPMaxReporting

Halved the cost of a lead by finding where budget was leaking

Home & garden supplier · US · lead generation

The problem. Six campaigns were all running out of budget daily, yet one of them was taking 42% of the account's ad views and producing almost nothing: four leads in seven months at $194 each.

What I did. Cut that campaign's budget, added negative keywords for competitor names and wrong-intent searches, and moved money toward stronger campaigns, starting with branded search at $8.92 a lead. I also rebuilt the monthly report into a plain-English one-pager so the owner could see where every dollar went.

Full breakdown
  • Segmented seven months of campaign data by month to find the weak spot instead of trimming budgets evenly.
  • Identified branded search as the cheapest lead source but capped at $5/day, and made the case to fund it.
  • Flagged that over half of all "leads" were phone-number taps, so the client didn't mistake them for connected calls.
  • Built a monthly budget table so the client could compare their own ad spend against the campaigns we ran.

CASE 02
SearchGA4Bidding

Rebuilt a flooring account that was spending with zero leads to show for it

Flooring retailer · US · showroom leads

The problem. Four of five legacy campaigns had spent over $2,000 between them without recording a single lead. Calls and form fills weren't being counted properly, so bidding had nothing to learn from.

What I did. Replaced the old structure with one campaign per service, fixed call and form conversion tracking, then switched bidding from Maximize Clicks to Maximize Conversions. Once the data proved which service worked, I wrote the budget recommendation the owner used to approve more spend.

Full breakdown
  • Service-specific campaigns for tile, hardwood, carpet and luxury vinyl, each with tailored ad copy and keyword sets.
  • Diagnosed a mid-month traffic drop as a budget ceiling, not lost demand, by matching Google Ads against GA4.
  • Recommended a phased increase led by the best campaign, reviewed against cost per lead at each step.
  • Added 82 new keywords to cover laminate and vinyl searches the account was missing.

CASE 04
B2BGTMGA4

Caught a lead count that was 14× higher than reality

Industrial manufacturer · international · B2B

The problem. The quarterly report showed nearly 400 conversions. Digging into GA4, one event had fired 382 times from 117 users while the site's form inbox held about 26 real enquiries. Page views were also being counted twice on every load.

What I did. Moved the inflated action to secondary, removed the duplicate page view in Tag Manager, and installed proper lead tracking that fires only on a successful form submission or a phone/email click. I added tracking-status slides to the quarterly deck so leadership could see exactly what the numbers meant.

Full breakdown
  • Traced 255 of the inflated events to two foreign-language landing pages whose form received one submission all quarter.
  • Explained a low sessions-per-click ratio: the cookie banner blocked analytics until visitors accepted.
  • Found the site had no clickable phone or email links, so those conversions could never have fired.
  • Kept paid traffic efficient while tracking was rebuilt: 7,479 clicks at $0.17 CPC and a 4.82% CTR for the quarter.

02 / APPROACHHow I work an account

The same four steps, whether it's a $1,000 local account or a multi-location brand.

01

Verify the tracking

Before touching bids, I check that every conversion is real, counted once and tied to a lead the client would recognize.

02

Find the leak

Break performance down by campaign and month to see which spend is working and which is just busy.

03

Restructure and reallocate

One service per campaign, tighter keywords, and budget moved to the proven cost per lead.

04

Report it plainly

Monthly and quarterly reports a business owner can read in two minutes, with the next steps spelled out.
