Finding the budget leak in a home & garden ad account

Home & garden supplier · United States · lead generation · Jan–Oct 2026

A supplier of stone veneer, hardscape and landscaping services was spending around $1,800 a month on Google Ads. Most campaigns were profitable, but one was quietly eating the account's visibility. This is how I found it, what I changed, and what the numbers did.

01 / THE SHORT VERSIONThree things to know

01

The account worked, but money sat in the wrong place

Five of six campaigns ran out of budget every day while one campaign took 42% of all ad views and produced 4 leads in seven months.

02

Budget moved to what converts

I cut the weak campaign's budget in half, blocked wrong-intent searches and moved the money to campaigns converting at 11% to 17%.

03

The owner could finally read the report

I rebuilt the monthly report into one plain-English page: what's working, what isn't, and what happens next.

02 / THE PROBLEMCheap clicks that never became customers

When I broke seven months of data down by campaign, the Maintenance campaign stood out. It had the cheapest clicks in the account at $1.09, so on a normal dashboard it looked efficient. It was also showing up for 42% of every ad impression in the account.

But it produced 4 leads from 709 clicks, a conversion rate of 0.56%. That works out to $193.77 per lead, against $8.92 for branded search and $16.23 for Performance Max.

The lesson

A low cost per click is not the goal. A $1.09 click that never converts costs more than a $4 click that converts one time in eight.

What else was holding the account back

  • Five of six campaigns were "Limited by budget", so the campaigns that did convert were switching off early each day.
  • $37 a day was parked in three paused campaigns (Events, Call and Remarketing), counted in the budget but doing nothing.
  • Google had disapproved most ads in two of the best campaigns, Hardscape and Branded.
  • Leads were sliding after April: from 101 in April to 57 in July, on roughly the same spend.

03 / WHAT I DIDStep by step

Step 1 · Audit

Broke the account down by campaign and month

Instead of trimming every budget evenly, I pulled every campaign's spend, clicks, leads and calls for each month from January to July. This showed which campaigns carried the account and which only looked busy.

  • Reviewed 18 months of history on the weakest campaign: about 25 leads at roughly $68 each, against an account average closer to $17.
  • Ranked every campaign by cost per lead, not cost per click.
Step 2 · Budget reallocation

Moved daily budget from the leak to a stronger campaign

I halved the weak campaign's budget and gave the money to a campaign that was converting and capped by budget.

$4/day→$2/dayMaintenance campaign · 0.56% conversion rate
$10/day→$12/dayVeneer campaign · 11.5% conversion rate, limited by budget

Maintenance spend dropped from about $120 a month to $59.52 in July once the change took effect.

Step 3 · Search term cleanup

Blocked the searches that were never going to convert

I went through the search terms that triggered the weak campaign and added six phrase-match negative keywords: two competitor names, plus four searches for things the business doesn't offer or that showed research rather than buying intent.

[competitor name] competitor [competitor name] competitor landscape supply wrong intent koi pond repair not offered patio paving ideas research can a sloped yard be leveled research
Step 4 · Reporting

Rebuilt the monthly report so the owner could act on it

The old report was a wall of numbers. I rebuilt the January to July report as a workbook with one plain-English summary page on top, and caught and fixed a chart error along the way where the legend showed numbers instead of campaign names.

  • What's working: Branded at $8.92 a lead, Performance Max at $16.23, Landscape picking up 71 leads from March.
  • What isn't: Maintenance at $194 a lead, and Veneer as the most expensive campaign still running.
  • One thing to know about the numbers: over half the leads are phone-number taps, which don't confirm a call connected, and conversion value is a placeholder, not job revenue.
Step 5 · Action plan

Set a clear, ranked plan for the next quarter

  • Fix the disapprovals on Hardscape and Branded, since nothing else works properly while ads are blocked.
  • Fund Branded, the cheapest lead source, which was capped at $5 a day.
  • Clean up or pause Maintenance and reuse its budget.
  • Investigate the post-April drop: competitors, seasonality and the website.
  • Review Veneer against Hardscape, which sells a similar product at a lower cost per lead.
Step 6 · Protecting the budget

Pushed back on spending more before the data supported it

When the client wanted to push the Maintenance campaign harder for the fall cleanup season, I checked the latest numbers first: about $556 over 42 days for 1 conversion, a 0.65% conversion rate against 9% to 17% on the other campaigns.

I recommended checking conversion tracking and landing page relevance before adding budget, and the push was put on hold until that check was done. More money into a campaign that doesn't convert only makes the leak bigger.

Step 7 · Budget transparency

Built a clear budget table for the monthly review

For the October review I built a budget table showing exactly what the managed campaigns cost and delivered, so the client could compare it against the ad spend they run themselves.

Why the cost per click went up

Average cost per click rose from $2.34 in January to $3.25 in July. That was expected: budget moved away from $1.09 clicks that rarely converted and toward Search campaigns where clicks cost around $4 but one in eight became a lead. Paying more per click to pay less per customer is the right trade.
