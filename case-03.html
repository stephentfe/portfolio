02 / THE PROBLEMAn $8K-a-month account that couldn't prove its results

A multi-location restaurant group ran around eight campaigns across its locations, catering, delivery and private events. When I audited the tracking, I found the brand spread across five separate websites: the main site, a catering mirror, an online retail shop and two food-ordering platforms.

  • Both ordering platforms had no Google tracking at all. Online food orders were invisible to the ad account.
  • The "Submit lead forms" goal had no working primary action. A Universal Analytics conversion that had stopped recording in 2023 was still marked primary, while the live form tracking sat unused as secondary.
  • A catering-quote event had never fired because stray characters had been saved into its page-path rule.
  • The Analytics property everyone reported on was the retail shop, not the restaurants.
  • Five of eight campaigns were limited by budget, while one campaign was spending $922 a month for 2 conversions.
Cost per conversion by campaign, September
Weddings & Events Search cost $460.88 per conversion. Most others were under $12.

03 / WHAT I DIDStep by step

Step 1 · Tracking audit

Mapped every website and what it measured

I traced each domain to its Analytics property and data stream. That showed the property used in reports was the retail shop, and that the restaurant ordering flows had no tracking.

Step 2 · Conversion fixes

Made the lead goal work again

  • Dead conversion demoted: the old Universal Analytics catering form moved from primary to secondary.
  • Live form tracking promoted: the working GA4 form_submit (19 conversions in two weeks) moved to primary, so "Submit lead forms" went from 0 primary actions to 1.
  • Broken event repaired: fixed the catering-quote page path so the event can fire.
  • Add to cart marked as a key event on the retail shop.
  • Two new GA4 data streams created for the ordering platforms, with the tag install handed to the platform owners.
Step 3 · Waste

Halved the private-events budget

Private-event search spent $921.77 in September for 2 conversions. Its top search terms were competitor venues and caterers. I halved its budget and split it into three tighter ad groups (wedding catering, rehearsals and showers, private events) using exact and phrase match only.

$30/day→$15/dayWeddings & Events Search · $460.88 per conversion in September
Step 4 · Launch

Launched the loyalty app campaign across every location

When the client launched a loyalty rewards app, I built a dedicated Performance Max campaign and added a loyalty asset group to all six other Performance Max campaigns, so every location promoted the app.

  • 15 headlines, 5 long headlines, 5 descriptions and 20 search themes, written from the client's spec sheet.
  • Resized the client's square graphic to 1200×628 for landscape placements without redrawing it.
  • Paused a late-night campaign and rewrote a location's entire asset group the same day the client dropped that offer.
Step 5 · Budget plan

Funded the launch inside the client's monthly cap

My first proposal added $1,684 a month (+19%). The client capped the increase at $400 on a $8,406 plan, so I reworked it: fund the loyalty app by cutting the weakest campaign and leave everything else unchanged.

$16.67/day→$65/dayLoyalty app PMax · Google's own recommendation was $63.32/day

The catch was a promo campaign that only runs on Tuesdays. A simple 30.4-day calculation overstated it by about $1,000 a month. Modelling it by Tuesdays showed the plan landing at $8,678 to $8,773 a month against the $8,806 cap, with the tightest month flagged in advance.

Step 6 · Promotions

Pushed the weekly price-point promo to every location

I copied the promo asset group into all three location campaigns, swapped in the new third-party graphics, removed two YouTube videos that had gone private and were blocking saves, and added two new catering videos to an asset group that had none.
